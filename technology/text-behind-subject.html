Words behind the subject, subjects out of the frame.

The magazine-cover effect, where a name sits behind someone’s shoulder, comes from separating the photo into ordered layers. Once the subject is its own layer, text can sit in front of the scene but behind the person, and the subject can break out past the frame.

The matte size remains fixed; 3D Pop gains room by shrinking the inner frame.
Terminology guardVisual layers describe what the customer sees. Software architecture layers describe responsibilities in the code. Stored document layers describe persisted creative structure. The three views should not be presented as one-to-one equivalents.

Common questions

How do I put text behind a person in a photo?

Add a caption, then set its depth to sit behind the subject. The app places the words above the background scene but underneath the separated subject, which produces the magazine-cover look.

What is 3D Pop?

The separated subject is allowed to cross the frame edge, so a head, hand or paw appears to break out of the frame while the rest of the photo stays inside it.

Why does behind-subject text need a clear subject?

The effect depends on the subject being separated from the background. Without a usable subject there is nothing for the words to sit behind, so the app explains that instead of faking the depth.
